Why Is India on UTC+05:30? The Half-Hour Time Zone Explained

The history and logic behind India's (and Sri Lanka's) half-hour offset, how it affects global teams, and tips for scheduling across IST.

Updated October 2026 ·

Most of the world's time zones are whole hours from UTC. India's isn't. IST is UTC+05:30, based on the 82.5°E meridian near Mirzapur. That longitude falls almost exactly between the UTC+5 and UTC+6 lines, and choosing the midpoint gave one national clock that fits both the far west (Gujarat) and the far east (Arunachal Pradesh) reasonably well.

Who else is on 05:30?

Sri Lanka (SLST) is also UTC+05:30. Nepal goes further, to UTC+05:45.

The trade-offs

  • The north-east gets sunrise very early by the clock. There are regular proposals for a second time zone there.
  • No daylight saving means the difference to Europe and North America changes twice a year, on their DST dates.

Scheduling tips for global teams

  • Europe ↔ India: late morning in Europe is mid-afternoon in India, the sweet spot.
  • US East ↔ India: 08:00–10:00 ET overlaps with India's evening; rotate the pain.
