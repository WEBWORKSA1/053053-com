One-Ring (Wangiri) Scams: Why You Must Never Call Back

How one-ring Wangiri scams work, which country codes they use, what a call-back costs, and how to protect yourself.

Updated October 2026 ·

Wangiri (Japanese for “one ring and cut”) is a fraud in which your phone rings once, often late at night, from an international number. The caller hangs up before you can answer, betting you'll call back. That call-back goes to a premium-rate or high-termination-fee line, and part of the charge goes to the fraudster.

How to recognise it

  • A single ring, or a call that drops the moment you answer
  • An unfamiliar country code: frequently African, Eastern European, Pacific or satellite codes (+881/+882/+883)
  • Caribbean codes that look domestic in North America: +1 876 (Jamaica), +1 809/829/849 (Dominican Republic), +1 473 (Grenada), +1 268 (Antigua), +1 649 (Turks & Caicos)
  • Several missed calls in a row from numbers that differ only in the last digits
  • A voicemail urging you to call back about a prize, parcel or “urgent family matter”
